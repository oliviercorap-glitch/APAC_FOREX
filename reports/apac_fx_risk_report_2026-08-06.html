1. FX Regimes by Currency

| Currency | Regime Type | Key Features | |----------|-------------|--------------| | CNY | PBOC-managed float (crawling peg to basket) | Daily central parity (midpoint) set at 9:15am SGT; trading band ±2% vs USD; band widened over time; spot rarely at band edges due to official guidance | | JPY | Free float with occasional MOF/BOJ intervention | No formal band; intervention (buy/sell USDJPY) rare but used at extremes; verbal intervention frequent | | KRW | Free float with BOK smoothing operations | No band; BOK acts to curb excess volatility, not to target a level; authorities use macroprudential measures (e.g., FX forward position limits) | | THB | Free float with BOT intervention | BOT intervenes to smooth sharp moves, builds reserves; occasional capital controls (e.g., 2010 withholding tax on bonds) | | PHP | Free float with BSP intervention | BSP active in spot market to manage volatility; remittance flows (OFW) are a structural anchor | | AUD | Fully free float, no intervention | RBA does not target level; rate determined by market; highly correlated with commodity terms of trade |

2. Central Bank Mandates & Policy Tools

PBOC (China) – Mandate: price stability, growth, financial stability. Tools: daily fixing, open market operations, reserve requirement ratio (RRR), window guidance to banks, direct spot intervention, macroprudential (countercyclical factor on fixing). Currency stability is a policy objective in itself; PBOC tolerates gradual depreciation but resists sharp moves.

BOJ (Japan) – Mandate: price stability (2% inflation target), financial stability. Tools: yield curve control (YCC) on 10Y JGBs, negative policy rate (historically), QQE (asset purchases), forward guidance. FX intervention is a Ministry of Finance (MOF) decision, executed by BOJ; BOJ's ultra-loose policy historically weakens JPY.

BOK (Korea) – Mandate: price stability, financial stability. Tools: base rate, open market operations, FX intervention (smoothing), macroprudential measures (caps on banks' FX derivatives). BOK is sensitive to KRW volatility due to export dependence and high household debt.

BOT (Thailand) – Mandate: price stability, financial stability, sustainable growth. Tools: policy rate, FX intervention, capital flow measures (e.g., bond withholding tax), reserve accumulation. BOT historically resists excessive THB strength to protect export competitiveness.

BSP (Philippines) – Mandate: price stability, financial stability. Tools: policy rate, RRR, FX intervention, occasional administrative measures. BSP manages PHP volatility around remittance and BPO inflows; inflation targeting since 2002.

RBA (Australia) – Mandate: price stability (2–3% inflation), full employment, economic welfare. Tools: cash rate target, QE (used 2020–21), forward guidance, no FX intervention. RBA explicitly does not target the AUD; policy is domestic-focused.

3. Structural Drivers vs USD/EUR

Rate Differentials: The single largest driver. When US/EU rates are high relative to APAC, capital flows out of APAC into USD/EUR assets, pressuring APAC currencies. JPY is most sensitive (carry trade unwind risk); AUD is a high-beta play on global risk appetite; CNY is driven by PBOC policy stance relative to Fed/ECB.

Capital Flows: Portfolio flows dominate short-term moves. Equity/bond inflows strengthen KRW, THB, PHP; outflows weaken them. China's capital controls limit but do not eliminate CNY pressure. FDI flows are structural for CNY and PHP (remittances).

Commodity Exposure (AUD): AUD is a proxy for global commodity prices (iron ore, coal, LNG). Terms-of-trade shifts drive AUD independently of rates. A China slowdown hits AUD via commodity demand.

Export Competitiveness Dynamics: Japan, Korea, Thailand, and China all have export-driven models. Their central banks tolerate or encourage weaker currencies to maintain competitiveness. This creates a "race to the bottom" bias in Asia, especially when global demand is soft. The CNY is the anchor – other Asian currencies often move in the same direction as CNY to preserve relative competitiveness.

EUR Crosses: EUR/APAC moves are driven by EURUSD plus USD/APAC. When EUR strengthens vs USD, APAC currencies typically weaken vs EUR (unless they strengthen more vs USD). For EUR-reporting entities, USD moves matter more than EUR moves for APAC exposure.

4. Practical Implications for a Manufacturing Exporter

Margin Exposure from Invoicing Mismatches: - You invoice in CNY, JPY, KRW, THB, PHP, AUD; costs are partly in EUR and partly local (APAC). - A weaker APAC currency vs EUR reduces EUR-equivalent revenue, squeezing margins unless local costs also fall (they rarely do proportionally). - USD as secondary reference: if you price in USD but invoice in local currency, USD/APAC moves create direct P&L volatility. - Key risk: APAC currencies are structurally biased weaker vs EUR over time (due to export competitiveness policies and rate differentials). This is a persistent margin drag, not just a cyclical risk.

Typical Hedging Instruments: - Forwards (NDFs for CNY, deliverable for others): Standard tool; lock in rates for 3–12 months. CNY NDFs are liquid; onshore CNY forwards require trade documentation. - Currency Swaps: Useful for longer-dated exposures (2–5 years) or when you need to swap cash flows (e.g., convert local revenue into EUR). - Natural Hedging: Match local currency revenues with local currency costs (e.g., source inputs locally, pay local wages in same currency). This reduces net exposure without derivatives. - Options (collars, strangles): For uncertain timing or to protect against adverse moves while retaining upside. More expensive than forwards but flexible.

Timing Considerations: - Hedge rolling: Do not hedge 100% for long periods; roll hedges quarterly to avoid locking in unfavorable rates for too long. - PBOC fixing times: CNY moves often occur around the 9:15am fixing; avoid executing large CNY trades during that window. - BOJ meetings and MOF intervention: JPY can gap on intervention announcements; avoid holding large unhedged JPY positions around these events. - Month-end/quarter-end: APAC currencies often show seasonal patterns around month-end (corporate demand, portfolio rebalancing). Plan hedging execution accordingly. - Election/policy events: BOK, BOT, BSP meetings are scheduled; RBA and BOJ too. Avoid large unhedged positions into these events.
