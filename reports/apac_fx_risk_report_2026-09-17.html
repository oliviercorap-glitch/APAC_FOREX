Structural brief (background context, updated weekly)

APAC FX Structural Background Note

For: APAC CFO, Western Manufacturing Group From: Macro FX Strategy – Asia-Pacific Re: Structural reading grid for CNY, JPY, KRW, THB, PHP, AUD exposure Scope: Background only. No recent news, no live rate levels.

1. FX Regimes

CNY – Managed float. The PBOC sets a daily central parity (fixing) against the USD, to which a permitted trading band around the fix applies in the onshore market (CNY). The fixing is influenced by a basket of currencies and by counter-cyclical and "reverse cycle" factors. A separate offshore market (CNH) trades more freely and often diverges from onshore CNY during stress. Capital account management (quotas, repatriation rules) remains a structural feature.

JPY – Free float. No band; intervention is rare and typically verbal or, occasionally, actual (MOF instructs, BOJ executes). JPY is a classic funding and safe-haven currency.

KRW – Free float. Market-determined, but the BOK and authorities have historically smoothed disorderly moves via verbal guidance and, at times, spot intervention. Korea is exposed to global risk appetite and semiconductor cycle flows.

THB – Free float. Managed in practice through smoothing operations and rules affecting capital flows (e.g., reporting thresholds on foreign holdings). Thailand runs persistent current account surpluses, which structurally support the THB.

PHP – Free float. The BSP intervenes mainly to smooth volatility, not to target a level. Remittance inflows and outsourcing revenues are structural supports.

AUD – Free float. One of the most liquid, freely traded currencies; no routine intervention. AUD is a commodity and risk-sentiment proxy.

2. Central Bank Mandates and Tools

  • PBOC: Multi-objective (growth, employment, price stability, financial stability, exchange rate stability). Currency tools: daily fixing, band management, FX reserves, capital-flow measures, offshore CNH liquidity.
  • BOJ: Price stability (2% target) with a yield-curve control legacy; FX is not its primary mandate but it coordinates with MOF on intervention.
  • BOK: Inflation targeting; FX smoothing is secondary. Tools: policy rate, verbal intervention, occasional spot operations, macroprudential FX rules.
  • BOT: Inflation targeting; explicitly permits intervention to curb excessive volatility. Tools: policy rate, FX smoothing, capital-flow reporting rules.
  • BSP: Price stability; FX smoothing to avoid disorderly moves. Tools: policy rate, spot intervention, FX forward facilities for corporates.
  • RBA: Inflation targeting; FX is market-determined, intervention only in exceptional circumstances. Tools: cash rate, forward guidance, occasional FX operations.

3. Structural Drivers vs USD/EUR

  • Rate differentials: USD and EUR policy rates versus APAC rates drive carry and forward points. Higher-yielding APAC currencies (e.g., PHP, THB at times) tend to trade at forward discounts versus USD/EUR; low-yielders (JPY, CNY) at premiums.
  • Capital flows: Portfolio and direct investment flows, plus global risk sentiment, dominate KRW, THB, PHP and AUD. China's capital account management insulates CNY partially.
  • Commodity exposure: AUD is structurally linked to iron ore, coal, and base metals; terms-of-trade shocks transmit directly to the currency. THB and PHP have softer commodity links (oil imports, gold, electronics).
  • Export competitiveness: CNY management has historically balanced competitiveness against appreciation pressure from trade surpluses. JPY weakness is often a by-product of monetary policy, not explicit targeting. KRW, THB, PHP and AUD are more market-clearing but sensitive to regional competitiveness dynamics.

4. Practical Implications for a Manufacturing Exporter

Margin exposure. Invoicing in CNY, JPY, KRW, THB, PHP and AUD while reporting in EUR creates a layered mismatch: (i) EUR versus each APAC currency, and (ii) USD as a secondary reference. Even if contracts are denominated in USD, the EUR/USD leg remains an unhedged exposure. CNY is the least freely convertible; hedging CNH is standard, but onshore CNY hedges require access to onshore instruments.

Hedging instruments. - Forward contracts: The workhorse for known receivables in all six currencies. Tenors should match expected collection dates. - FX swaps: Useful for rolling hedges and managing liquidity; CNH and JPY swaps are deep, KRW/THB/PHP less so. - Natural hedging: Match costs and revenues in the same currency where possible (e.g., local sourcing in CNY, JPY, KRW, THB, PHP, AUD). This reduces the notional to hedge. - Options: For uncertain volumes or tender situations; AUD and JPY options are liquid, PHP and THB less so.

Timing considerations. - CNY: watch the daily fixing and any changes to the band or counter-cyclical factors; onshore/offshore basis can widen around quarter-ends and policy events. - JPY: intervention risk is asymmetric; avoid large unhedged positions around MOF/BOJ communication. - KRW, THB, PHP: hedge around known flow events (month-end, remittance cycles, tourism seasons) and central bank meetings. - AUD: commodity price and China data sensitivity; hedge terms-of-trade-driven volatility.

Governance. Set a hedge ratio policy per currency, define permissible instruments, and stress-test EUR and USD legs separately. Given CNY's managed regime, treat it as a distinct risk bucket from the free-floaters.
