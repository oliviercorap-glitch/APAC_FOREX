APAC FX Structural Background Note

For: APAC CFO, Western Manufacturing Group Re: Structural FX framework for CNY, JPY, KRW, THB, PHP, AUD exposure Purpose: Background reading grid (no recent news or live rates)

1. FX Regimes

CNY (Chinese yuan): A managed float. The PBOC sets a daily central parity (the "fix") against USD, historically referencing a basket (CFETS, BIS, SDR) with a counter-cyclical adjustment factor. The onshore rate (USD/CNY) trades within a band around the fix (widened over time to ±2%). A separate offshore market (CNH) floats more freely and often diverges from onshore, creating a basis relevant to hedging. Capital account management (quotas, repatriation rules) remains a structural feature.

JPY (Japanese yen): Free-floating, no band. The MOF directs intervention (via the BOJ) only in exceptional cases; the BOJ's primary lever is monetary policy, not the exchange rate. JPY is a classic funding and safe-haven currency.

KRW (Korean won): Free-floating. The BOK and MOF intervene to smooth disorderly moves ("smoothing operations") but do not target a level. KRW is highly sensitive to global risk appetite and semiconductor/export cycles.

THB (Thai baht): Free-floating. The BOT intervenes to curb excessive volatility, not to set a level, and has periodically used measures to moderate short-term capital inflows.

PHP (Philippine peso): Free-floating. The BSP intervenes to smooth volatility and manage inflation pass-through; it is one of Asia's more active "smoothers."

AUD (Australian dollar): Free-floating since 1983. The RBA does not target a level and intervenes only rarely (chiefly to address extreme illiquidity). AUD is a commodity/risk-proxy currency.

2. Central Bank Mandates and Tools

  • PBOC: Multi-objective (growth, employment, price stability, balance of payments). Tools: daily fix, trading band, reserve requirements, policy rates (LPR/MLF), window guidance, and direct FX reserves management.
  • BOJ: Price stability (~2% target). Tools: policy rate, yield curve control (historically), QQE, and FX reserves. Intervention is a MOF decision.
  • BOK: Price stability and financial stability. Tools: base rate, macroprudential measures, and smoothing operations via reserves.
  • BOT: Price stability and financial stability. Tools: policy rate, FX intervention, and capital-flow measures.
  • BSP: Price stability and financial stability. Tools: policy rate, FX intervention, and reserve management.
  • RBA: Price stability (2–3% band) and full employment. Tools: cash rate, bond purchases (historically), and rare FX intervention.

3. Structural Drivers vs USD/EUR

  • Rate differentials: USD and EUR policy rates versus APAC rates drive carry and forward points. Higher USD rates typically pressure CNY, JPY, KRW, THB, PHP; a weak JPY often reflects low domestic yields.
  • Capital flows: Portfolio and FDI flows into/out of Asia are pro-cyclical. Risk-off episodes strengthen USD and weaken most APAC currencies (JPY often excepted as a haven).
  • Commodity exposure: AUD is structurally linked to iron ore, coal, and base metals; terms-of-trade shocks move it sharply. THB and PHP have softer commodity links (oil imports, remittances for PHP).
  • Export competitiveness: CNY, JPY, KRW, THB, PHP all have export-sector sensitivity; competitive devaluation dynamics can appear in stress periods, though explicit targeting is rare.
  • EUR cross: For a EUR reporter, USD/APAC moves and EUR/USD moves compound. EUR strength versus USD can offset APAC weakness, and vice versa.

4. Practical Implications for a Manufacturing Exporter

Margin exposure: Invoicing in CNY, JPY, KRW, THB, PHP, AUD while reporting in EUR creates a two-layer mismatch: (i) APAC currency vs USD, and (ii) USD vs EUR. Even a "stable" APAC currency can produce EUR margin swings via EUR/USD.

Hedging instruments: - Forward contracts: Standard for CNY (onshore/offshore), JPY, KRW, THB, PHP, AUD. Tenors typically 1–12 months; liquidity varies (CNH and JPY deepest, PHP/THB thinner). - FX swaps: Useful for funding and rolling hedges; common in CNY, JPY, KRW. - Natural hedging: Match costs and revenues in the same currency where possible (e.g., local sourcing in CNY/JPY/KRW/THB). - Options: For uncertain volumes or to cap downside while retaining upside; more expensive in thin markets.

Timing considerations: - CNY: watch the daily fix and band; onshore/offshore basis affects hedge pricing. - JPY/KRW: sensitive to risk sentiment and US yields; hedge around data/central bank events. - THB/PHP: intervention can cap sharp moves; liquidity is thinner, so stagger hedges. - AUD: commodity and China-growth sensitive; hedge terms-of-trade shocks. - EUR/USD: the dominant residual risk for a EUR reporter; consider hedging at the EUR/USD level in addition to APAC legs.

Governance: Set a hedge policy (ratio, tenor, instruments), monitor the onshore/offshore CNY basis, and stress-test EUR margin under combined APAC and EUR/USD shocks.
