APAC FX Structural Background Note

1. FX Regimes

CNY — Managed float. The PBOC sets a daily central parity (fixing) against USD, derived from a basket of currencies with a counter-cyclical adjustment factor. The onshore spot (USD/CNY) trades within a ±2% band around the fix. Offshore CNH trades freely and can diverge from onshore CNY, creating a basis that matters for hedging and settlement. Capital account management (quotas, repatriation rules) remains a structural feature.

JPY — Free float. No band; intervention is rare and typically verbal or, occasionally, actual (MOF instructs BOJ). The yen is a funding currency and a safe-haven proxy, so it often moves inversely to risk sentiment and rate differentials.

KRW — Free float. No formal band. The BOK and authorities intervene to smooth disorderly moves, particularly around the won's role as a high-beta proxy for global risk and semiconductor-cycle sentiment. Onshore/offshore (NDF) markets interact closely.

THB — Free float. Managed against a trade-weighted basket in practice, with the BOT intervening to curb excessive volatility. Thailand runs persistent current-account surpluses and large FX reserves, which structurally support the baht but also attract official smoothing to protect exporters and tourism.

PHP — Free float. The BSP intervenes mainly to smooth volatility, not to target a level. The peso is driven by remittances, BPO receipts, import demand, and external debt service.

AUD — Free float. No intervention in normal conditions. The RBA has historically only intervened in extreme dislocation. AUD is a commodity/risk proxy, closely linked to China growth, iron ore, and terms of trade.

2. Central Bank Mandates and Tools

| Bank | Mandate focus | Currency-relevant tools | |---|---|---| | PBOC | Price stability, growth, financial stability | Daily fix, band, reserve requirements, window guidance, capital-flow management, FX reserves | | BOJ | Price stability (2% target) | Policy rate, YCC/QQE legacy, FX intervention only via MOF instruction | | BOK | Price stability, financial stability | Policy rate, macroprudential measures, smoothing operations, FX swap lines | | BOT | Price stability, exchange-rate stability | Policy rate, intervention, capital-flow measures, reserve accumulation | | BSP | Price stability, financial stability | Policy rate, FX intervention, reserve management, remittance monitoring | | RBA | Price stability, full employment | Policy rate, forward guidance; intervention only in extreme cases |

3. Structural Drivers vs USD/EUR

  • Rate differentials: USD and EUR policy rates relative to APAC rates drive carry flows. JPY and THB are historically low-yield; KRW, PHP, and AUD are higher-beta.
  • Capital flows: Portfolio and FDI flows into APAC are sensitive to US yields, China growth, and risk appetite. CNY and KRW are most exposed to shifts in global risk.
  • Commodity exposure: AUD is structurally linked to iron ore, coal, and China demand. THB and PHP have softer commodity links; JPY is a net importer, so higher oil hurts the trade balance.
  • Export competitiveness: CNY management balances export support against capital outflow pressure. JPY weakness is often a competitiveness issue for Korean and Thai exporters. AUD strength can hurt non-resource exporters.
  • EUR cross: For a EUR reporter, USD/APAC moves matter twice — once versus USD, once via EUR/USD. EUR strength can mechanically reduce EUR value of USD-linked or USD-pegged receipts.

4. Practical Implications for a Manufacturing Exporter

Margin exposure. Invoicing in CNY, JPY, KRW, THB, PHP, and AUD while reporting in EUR creates a multi-layer mismatch: APAC currency vs USD, then USD vs EUR. Even a "stable" CNY can move via the fix and band; JPY and AUD can be volatile. Margin risk is highest where invoicing currency differs from cost currency.

Hedging instruments. - Forwards: Standard for CNY (onshore and offshore), JPY, KRW (NDF), THB, PHP, AUD. Tenors typically 1–12 months, extendable. - FX swaps: Useful for liquidity and rolling hedges; common in CNY and KRW. - Natural hedging: Match receipts and costs in the same currency where possible (e.g., local sourcing in CNY or THB). - Options: Collars and participations for uncertain volumes or budget-rate protection. - NDFs: For KRW and CNY where onshore access is restricted.

Timing considerations. - Hedge ratios should reflect forecast confidence and margin sensitivity. - CNY fix and band behavior can create predictable intraday/onward patterns; avoid leaving large exposures unhedged around fixing windows. - JPY and AUD are sensitive to risk sentiment and commodity news; layer hedges rather than timing a single entry. - PHP and THB can be affected by remittance and tourism seasonality. - For EUR reporting, consider hedging EUR/USD separately from USD/APAC to isolate the two legs.

Governance. Document hedge policy, permitted instruments, counterparty limits, and mark-to-market reporting. Given the mix of free floats and managed regimes, a single "APAC hedge ratio" is less effective than currency-specific policies.
