APAC FX Structural Background Note

For: APAC CFO, Western Manufacturing Group Reporting currency: EUR | Secondary reference: USD Invoice currencies: CNY, JPY, KRW, THB, PHP, AUD

1. FX Regimes by Currency

| Currency | Regime Type | Key Features | |----------|-------------|--------------| | CNY | PBOC-managed float (crawling peg to basket) | Daily central parity (midpoint) set at 9:15am; trading band ±2% vs USD; occasional "fixing" signals policy intent | | JPY | Free float | No formal band; MOF/BOJ intervene sporadically (more often on excessive volatility than level) | | KRW | Free float with smoothing operations | BOK/FSS intervene to curb one-sided moves; capital flow management measures (macroprudential) | | THB | Free float with active BOT management | BOT leans against excessive volatility; occasional capital controls on short-term inflows | | PHP | Free float with BSP intervention | BSP smooths volatility; historically managed against USD; remittance flows dominate | | AUD | Pure free float | No intervention; fully market-determined; one of the most liquid commodity currencies |

2. Central Bank Mandates & Policy Tools

PBOC (China) - Mandate: price stability, growth, financial stability; exchange rate is a policy tool, not a target per se. - Tools: daily fixing, reserve requirement ratio (RRR) on FX deposits, countercyclical factor in fixing, window guidance to state banks, offshore (CNH) liquidity management.

BOJ (Japan) - Mandate: price stability (2% inflation target); FX intervention is MOF's decision, BOJ executes. - Tools: yield curve control (YCC) on JGBs, negative policy rate (historically), outright JGB purchases; intervention via selling/buying USD/JPY.

BOK (Korea) - Mandate: price stability, financial stability; explicit FX smoothing mandate. - Tools: policy rate, FX swap lines with US Fed, forward position limits for banks, macroprudential levies on non-core FX liabilities.

BOT (Thailand) - Mandate: monetary stability, financial system stability; manages baht to support external competitiveness. - Tools: policy rate, FX intervention (sterilised), limits on non-resident baht accounts, bond withholding tax on foreign holders.

BSP (Philippines) - Mandate: price stability, financial stability; peso stability is a stated objective. - Tools: policy rate, direct FX intervention, reserve requirement variations, BSP-issued securities.

RBA (Australia) - Mandate: price stability, full employment, economic welfare; no FX target. - Tools: cash rate, forward guidance, QE (used in crisis); FX intervention only in extreme disorderly conditions (rare).

3. Structural Drivers vs USD/EUR

Rate Differentials - JPY: Structural low rates; sensitive to US-Japan yield spreads. When US yields rise, JPY weakens (carry outflow). - AUD: Rate differential vs US/EU matters, but commodity terms of trade dominate. - CNY/KRW/THB/PHP: Rate differentials matter, but capital flow controls (CNY, THB) and export competitiveness concerns (KRW) dampen pure carry dynamics.

Capital Flows - CNY: Portfolio inflows/outflows via Stock Connect, Bond Connect; FDI remains large. Equity market sentiment drives short-term moves. - KRW/THB: Highly sensitive to global risk appetite (equity/bond flows); both are "high-beta" Asian currencies. - PHP: Remittance inflows (≈9% of GDP) provide structural support; BSP accumulates reserves. - AUD: Capital flows tied to mining investment and sovereign wealth flows; also a proxy for China growth expectations.

Commodity Exposure (AUD) - AUD is a proxy for iron ore, coal, LNG, and gold prices. Terms-of-trade shifts drive multi-year trends. - A China slowdown (property/infrastructure) directly hits AUD via commodity demand.

Export Competitiveness Dynamics - CNY: PBOC historically resists sharp appreciation to protect exporters; gradual appreciation path preferred. - KRW: BOK/SMOF tolerate depreciation to support chaebol exporters; intervention only to slow pace. - THB: BOT sensitive to baht strength vs regional peers; tourism recovery adds structural inflow. - JPY: Weak yen is broadly accepted as supportive of corporate earnings; MOF only acts on disorderly moves.

EUR Cross-Rates - EUR/Asia pairs are driven by (a) USD/Asia direction, (b) EUR/USD direction. When EUR strengthens vs USD, Asian currencies typically weaken vs EUR (unless they strengthen even more vs USD). - For EUR-reporting CFO: the EUR/CNY, EUR/JPY, EUR/AUD crosses are the true exposure; USD is only a transmission channel.

A 5% move in CNY/EUR or JPY/EUR directly hits EBITDA margins if costs are in EUR or USD. - Key risk: Asian currencies tend to weaken together vs EUR when global risk-off hits; diversification across the six currencies reduces but does not eliminate aggregate exposure. - AUD is the outlier: It can move independently (commodity shocks) and may act as a partial hedge against CNY weakness (if China slowdown hits both, correlation rises).
